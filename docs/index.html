Health & sleep

In the evening, your eyes expect warm light.

Screen light is rich in blue, like daylight. In the evening, it sends your brain a “daytime” signal that can suppress melatonin, the hormone that prepares you for sleep.

Your sleep rhythm

Our internal clock is set by light. Warm light in the evening goes along with the natural drop in alertness.

Eye strain

Hours in front of a very bright screen tire the eyes. A softer tint and lower brightness make evening reading more comfortable.

The recommendations

Sleep specialists advise limiting screen exposure before bed. When you can't avoid it, warming the light is a good habit.

FAQ

Good to know.

How is it different from Night Shift?

Sereneye goes further: a much warmer tint (down to 1900 K), brightness below the macOS minimum, automatic pause for your photo apps, custom hours and a keyboard shortcut.

Does it work with an external display?

Yes. The filter applies to every connected display and comes back automatically when you plug in a new one.

Do I need a subscription?

No. After the 7-day free trial, a one-time “All Time” purchase of €2.99 unlocks Sereneye forever, on all your Macs.

Which version of macOS do I need?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Which languages is Sereneye available in?

The app follows your Mac's language and is available in 12 languages: English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian and Turkish.

How do I contact support?

Write to contact@sereneye.com, or visit the support page. We reply within 48 hours.
